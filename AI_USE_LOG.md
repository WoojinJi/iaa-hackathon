# AI Use and Verification Log

How AI was used in the workflow, how each AI output was checked, and what changed as a result.
Every AI suggestion was treated as a hypothesis until verified against the data or the competition
documentation. Entries link to `DECISIONS.md` and notebook sections.

**Types**
- **A** – AI proposed; verified by the team with data
- **H** – Team member questioned or challenged; AI analysis followed
- **E** – AI error, caught by review or a sanity check

## Lessons so far
- **Sanity checks on outputs caught AI code errors** (entry 7). Plausibility of numbers is checked before interpreting them.
- **Manual recalculation found a data issue the AI had not looked for** (entry 9).
- **About half of AI's explanations were rejected by data** (entries 12, 16). Hypotheses are tested, not accepted.
- **Where data cannot decide, the assumption is stated and its impact quantified** (entry 10).
- **Asking AI "what was your criterion?" exposed judgments presented as findings** (entries 20, 22). Each was then tested or made consistent.
- **AI's own diagnostic rules are tested before use** (entry 29), not only its numbers. The test showed AI's rule was half right and changed how we use the diagnostics.
- **Rules found after looking at a test result are kept as hypotheses** (entry 29), to avoid fitting the rule to the test.
- **Listing assumptions surfaced issues that had been raised and then forgotten** (entry 24).
- **The team's domain reasoning set the checks AI had not thought of** (entries 64–66): elimination of causes (mix → season → man-made), splitting at the break date, seasonality as a source of false signals, and how renewals enter the target.
- **A tool is tested on known answers before it is trusted** (entry 64): the first scan failed its positive control and was fixed before its results were used.
- **AI over-stated the size of a real effect** (entry 67); the out-of-time test, not the explanation, decided.
- **A second model reviewed the assumptions independently** (entries 58–59). Its findings were tested, not accepted: the two largest were rebutted with data, and the test produced a new finding.
- **Platform rules are checked before strategy** (entry 61). AI assumed a common Kaggle setting that did not apply.
- **The evaluation metric can reward worse pricing** (entry 62); model choice also weighs the tails.
- **A setting that is "the same" can mean something different** (entry 83): the same alpha on a different deviance scale flattened two models; a known raw result exposed it.
- **Validate before submitting, even an experiment** (entry 85): the validation would have predicted E9's loss.
- **Structure beat capacity** (entries 84, 88): the strongest black box tied our model; the gain came from splitting frequency and severity. AI had also over-estimated the effort of the split; asking "can't we just run it?" settled it.
- **Literature is cited from the paper, not from AI's summary** (entry 90).
